# [The strange relationship between birthdays, wedding days and rain](https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2026/01/19/when-is-it-least-likely-rain-united-states/) #
This story from the Washington Post's Department of Data is far from hard-hitting, but it's interesting nonetheless. I enjoyed that Van Dam and Noll went beyond the two simple statistics in the beginning of the story (dry days vs. birthdays). Saying that late September is the least rainy time of year is one thing, but by exploring the data in each area of the country, they created a much clearer picture of what weather looks like in different regions. I also thought the maps they used were well-made and easy to understand, and they explored enough variables to make it a complex story.

If I could suggest a change, it would be to dive more deeply into the other part of the story -- birthdays. They only showed when the most common birthdays are across the country, but I would have liked to see how that data changes across the country. I'm not sure if that data exists, but it would have been interesting to see if it would line up with the regional weather data they explored.
