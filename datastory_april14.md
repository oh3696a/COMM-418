# [ICE moved detainees out of an overcrowded Mesa facility before congressional oversight visit](https://azmirror.com/2026/04/09/ice-moved-detainees-out-of-an-overcrowded-mesa-facility-before-congressional-oversight-visit/) # 

This story from the AZ Mirror starts with a single statistical anomaly: before a congressional oversight visit, ICE shuttled hundreds of people out of a detention facility by the Mesa Airport. Before the visit, the facility was far more packed than the stated max occupancy of 157 people, but it suddenly dipped to under capacity before the visit and shot back up right after.

I'm a big fan of a story that explores the implications of a single statistical trend, so I immediately liked this one. The author, Jared McDonald-Evoy, builds off of the statistic of the ICE facility's population and paints a picture of secrecy and mismanagement within several detention centers across the country. I also like the visualizations within the story; I think they're clear and don't get too fancy, and he uses the right types of charts to portray his points.

I'm not sure if this would even be possible, but I think one thing that would have made this story better is speaking to someone who was held at one of these facilities. There are a lot of expert perspectives, but an on-the-ground perspective would give an even better picture of how ICE is managing these facilities.
